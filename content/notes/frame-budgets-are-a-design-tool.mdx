A frame budget is usually introduced as a constraint you apologise for. It is
more useful as a design brief.

## Start from the budget, not the design

If you decide up front that a scene gets 8 ms of GPU time on a four-year-old
laptop, the question stops being "can we afford a reflective floor" and becomes
"what is the best thing we can buy with 2 ms." That reframing kills a lot of
arguments before they start.

## Measure the device you do not own

The devices that matter are the ones you do not have on your desk. Three
proxies get you most of the way:

- `navigator.hardwareConcurrency` for rough CPU class
- `navigator.deviceMemory` for whether textures will thrash
- the unmasked `RENDERER` string for the software-rasteriser case

None of them are precise. Together they are enough to pick a starting tier, and
a runtime monitor fixes the guess within a few seconds.

## Degrade in the right order

Things worth cutting, cheapest effect on perceived quality first:

1. Multisampling. Bloom hides the edges anyway.
2. Realtime shadows. Bake once and freeze the map.
3. Screen-space reflections or reflective floors.
4. Post-processing, all of it.
5. Geometry and particle counts.

Resolution comes last. Dropping device pixel ratio is the most visible cut you
can make, so it should be the one you reach for only when everything else is
already gone.
